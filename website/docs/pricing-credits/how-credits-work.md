# How Credits Work

Kite uses credits to pay for the work it does. Every conversation you have with Kite and every task it runs draws from your credits. This page explains what uses credits and how to make yours go further.

## What uses credits

Credits are consumed by Kite's work, not by signing in or reading what it has posted. In general:

- **Conversations** in Slack or the editor use credits each time Kite responds.
- **Tasks** use credits: research, drafting a page or an email, finding conversations on Reddit, proposing a conversion fix, building or editing your website.
- **Complex work** (a full growth plan, a multi-page build, a large rewrite) uses more than a small change.
- **Retries and troubleshooting** use credits too. If Kite has to try something again or fix an error, that is additional work.

:::info
Reading Kite's posts in Slack, viewing your site preview, and publishing are not the same as asking Kite to do work. It is the research, drafting, and editing that draws down credits.
:::

## Tips to use credits efficiently

1. **Give Kite context early.** Correcting its discovery research in the first week saves credits on every plan that builds on it.
2. **Be clear and specific.** "Change the pricing page headline to 'Scheduling that saves your front desk an hour a day'" works better than "make the top nicer." Specific requests get the right result the first time, so Kite does not have to retry.
3. **Make one change at a time.** Smaller, focused requests are easier for Kite to get right and easier for you to review before moving on.
4. **Describe the outcome you want,** not the steps. Tell Kite what the finished page or email should achieve.
5. **Review before you re-request.** If a draft looks close, ask for a specific revision rather than a full do-over.

## When Kite loops or repeats failed actions

Occasionally Kite gets stuck: retrying the same action, failing repeatedly, or looping. Because retries use credits, a loop can quietly burn through them.

If you notice Kite repeating itself or failing the same step over and over:

1. **Stop.** Do not keep sending the same request and hoping it works. Repeating the request usually repeats the loop.
2. **Rephrase.** Try describing the work differently, or break it into smaller pieces. A fresh, simpler request often gets past whatever Kite was stuck on.
3. **Undo if needed.** If the loop left your site in a messy state, roll back to a working version. See [Version History](/troubleshooting/version-history).
4. **Contact support.** If Kite keeps looping no matter how you phrase the request, reach out to support. Repeated, unproductive loops are not something you should have to pay for, and the team can help.

:::warning
If you see Kite clearly stuck in a loop, pause rather than pushing through. Stopping early protects your credits.
:::

## Common questions

**Does reading Kite's Slack posts or publishing my site use credits?**
No. Credits are for the work Kite does, not for viewing or publishing. <!-- TODO: verify with product -->

**Why did a small-sounding change use a lot of credits?**
"Small" to you can be large for Kite. Changes that touch many parts of a page, or that required retries, use more.

**I ran out of credits mid-project. What now?**
You can top up at any time. See [Buy More Credits](/pricing-credits/buy-credits).

**Credits got used up by a loop that was not my fault. Can that be fixed?**
Contact support and explain what happened. See the looping steps above.
