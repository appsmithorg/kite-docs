# How Kite Works

Kite is your AI marketer in Slack. It researches your business, builds a growth strategy, and ships the work that brings you new customers. Here are the core ideas that make everything else in these docs make sense.

## Kite finds the work, your team approves it

Most AI products wait for instructions. Kite looks at your business, finds what needs doing, and brings it to your Slack channel with the evidence behind it. Your team then decides. Kite works in a repeating loop:

1. **Research.** Kite studies your product, your customers, your competitors, and what AI answer engines say about you.
2. **Plan.** Kite turns that research into a growth strategy and posts it in Slack for review.
3. **Draft.** Kite produces the work itself: a comparison page, an outreach plan, a conversion fix on an existing page, a new section of your website.
4. **Approve.** Someone on your team reviews the draft in Slack and approves it, asks for changes, or declines.
5. **Ship.** Kite publishes only the work you approved, then tracks how it performs and feeds what it learns into the next plan.

## Nothing goes live without your approval

This is the most important rule in Kite. Every post, page, email, and site change arrives in Slack as a draft with a preview first. Kite does not publish anything until a member of your team approves it. If you decline, Kite drops the work or revises it. See **[How approvals work](/slack/approvals)**.

## Slack and the browser editor

Kite has two places where you interact with it, and they serve different purposes:

- **Slack** is where Kite brings you research, recommendations, and ready-to-review work, and where your team approves it. Most of your time with Kite happens here.
- **The browser editor** is where you make hands-on changes to your website: describe an edit in chat, click an element to change it, preview the result, and publish. It is also where you manage billing and your account. See **[Website Building](/website-building)**.

## Preview versus live

Work Kite drafts is not the same as work that is live. In the website editor there are two versions of your site:

- **Preview**: what you see as you work. Edits appear here right away.
- **Live (published)**: what your visitors see on the web.

Changes only reach visitors after they are approved and published. If your live site looks out of date, the change probably has not been published yet. See **[My changes aren't showing](/publishing/changes-not-showing)**.

## Credits

Every conversation you have with Kite and every task it runs uses **credits**. Research, drafting, site edits, and retries all draw from the same pool. Bigger or more complex work uses more. Clear, specific requests help you get more from each credit. See **[How credits work](/pricing-credits/how-credits-work)**.

## Undo and version history

Made a site change you do not like? Kite keeps checkpoints so you can preview and roll back to an earlier version. See **[Undo changes & version history](/troubleshooting/version-history)**.

## When something goes wrong

If a preview will not load or the editor freezes, it is usually quick to recover. Start with **[Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting/)**.
