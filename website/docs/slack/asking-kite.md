# Asking Kite to Do Something

Kite offers work on its own, and it does whatever you ask. This page covers how to make a request and how to give feedback that gets you a better result.

## Make a request

Mention Kite in a channel, or send it a direct message, and describe what you want in plain language. For example:

> @Kite draft a comparison page against Acme Scheduling, focused on clinics with more than one location.

> @Kite our sign-ups from the pricing page dropped last week. Find out why and fix it.

> @Kite add a testimonials section to the homepage using the three quotes in this thread.

Kite replies in a thread, does the research or drafting, and posts the result there. In channels set to follow threads automatically, you do not need to tag Kite again inside a thread it is already part of.

## What makes a good request

- **Say what outcome you want, not the steps.** "Get more clinic managers to book a demo from the pricing page" is better than a list of edits.
- **Name the audience.** Kite writes differently for a founder reading a newsletter than for an IT buyer reading a product page.
- **Point at specifics.** Name the page, the section, the competitor, or the channel.
- **Share what you already have.** Paste quotes, data, or links in the thread. Kite works from them.
- **One request per thread.** Keeping each piece of work in its own thread makes feedback and results easy to follow.

## Give feedback

When a draft is close but not right, reply in the thread with what to change:

> Keep the structure, but lead with the time saved at the front desk instead of the feature list.

> Shorter. Cut the second paragraph and end on the call to action.

Kite revises and posts again. Specific feedback gets better revisions than "make it better."

## Ask questions

You can also ask Kite about your business or its own work:

> @Kite what did the comparison page do for traffic this week?

> @Kite who are the top three competitors you found, and why those?
