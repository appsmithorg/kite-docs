# Asking Kite to Do Something

Kite finds work on its own, but you can also ask it for specific work at any time. This page covers how to make a request in Slack and how to give feedback that gets you a better result.

## Make a request

Mention Kite in the channel and describe what you want in plain language. For example:

> @Kite draft a comparison page against Acme Scheduling, focused on clinics with more than one location.

> @Kite our sign-ups from the pricing page dropped last week. Can you look into why and propose a fix?

> @Kite add a testimonials section to the homepage using the three quotes in this thread.

Kite replies in a thread, does the research or drafting, and posts the result for approval like any other work. See [How approvals work](/slack/approvals).

## What makes a good request

- **Say what outcome you want, not the steps.** "Get more clinic managers to book a demo from the pricing page" is better than a list of edits.
- **Name the audience.** Kite writes differently for a founder on Reddit than for an IT buyer reading a product page.
- **Point at specifics.** Name the page, the section, the competitor, or the channel.
- **Share what you already have.** Paste quotes, data, or links in the thread. Kite can work from them.
- **One request per thread.** Keeping each piece of work in its own thread makes approvals and revisions easy to follow.

## Give feedback

When a draft is close but not right, reply in the thread with what to change:

> Keep the structure, but lead with the time saved at the front desk instead of the feature list.

> Shorter. Cut the second paragraph and end on the call to action.

Kite revises and posts again. Specific feedback gets better revisions than "make it better."

## Ask questions

You can also ask Kite about your business or its own work:

> @Kite what did the comparison page do for traffic this week?

> @Kite who are the top three competitors you found, and why those?

## Credits

Every conversation with Kite and every task it runs uses credits. A clear request that Kite gets right the first time uses fewer credits than several rounds of revision. See [How credits work](/pricing-credits/how-credits-work).
